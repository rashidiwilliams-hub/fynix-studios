10 Healthy Lunch Ideas You'll Love

Quick, satisfying lunches that don't skimp on flavor or nutrition.

Quick, satisfying lunches that don't skimp on flavor or nutrition. Start your week with grain bowls loaded with quinoa, roasted vegetables, and a bright lemon dressing — they hold up well in the fridge and reheat easily. Wraps with grilled chicken, hummus, and crunchy greens are another go-to for busy days. Don't underestimate a good soup either; a hearty lentil or vegetable soup can be just as filling as anything heavier. The key across all of these is balancing protein, fiber, and healthy fats so you stay full until dinner.

June 5, 2024 · Cooking Tips

5 Cooking Tips From Our Chefs

Small habits that make a big difference in the kitchen.

Small habits that make a big difference in the kitchen. First, always taste as you go — seasoning at the end is too late to fix an underseasoned dish. Second, let your pan get properly hot before adding protein; it's the difference between a sear and a steam. Third, rest meat after cooking so the juices redistribute instead of running out onto the cutting board. Fourth, keep your knives sharp — a dull knife is both slower and more dangerous. Finally, prep your ingredients before you start cooking (mise en place); it turns a stressful cook into a calm one.

June 1, 2024 · Desserts

Delicious Desserts You Can Make At Home

Simple, crowd-pleasing desserts worth the effort.

Simple, crowd-pleasing desserts worth the effort. A good pine tart takes patience but very little skill — the key is stewing the pineapple down until it's thick and tangy before it ever touches the pastry. Chocolate lava cake is deceptively simple: slightly underbake it so the center stays molten. If you want something no-bake, a berry parfait layered with yogurt and granola comes together in minutes. Whatever you choose, use good-quality chocolate and real vanilla — it makes a noticeable difference in a dessert this simple.

May 22, 2024 · Recipes

The Secret to a Perfect Margherita Pizza

Three ingredients, done right, beat ten ingredients done average.

Three ingredients, done right, beat ten ingredients done average. Start with San Marzano tomatoes crushed by hand, not a food processor — you want texture, not puree. Use fresh mozzarella, torn rather than shredded, and add it partway through baking so it doesn't overcook. Bake as hot as your oven allows; pizza wants intense, fast heat to get that leopard-spotted crust. Finish with fresh basil and a drizzle of good olive oil right out of the oven, never before.
